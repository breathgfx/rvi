Internal migration and antenatal care continuity in three Tanzanian regions, 2016–2023

Rutasha Dadi, Hawa Mwakalinga, Joseph Sanga, Amina Kimaro

Download PDF · 1.4 MB Publisher version

Women who change district during pregnancy attend substantially fewer antenatal visits than those who stay, and the shortfall is not explained by how far they live from a facility. It is explained by how care is recorded, transferred and paid for.

Abstract

Using a three-round panel of 4,812 women interviewed in Dar es Salaam, Mbeya and Mwanza between 2016 and 2023, we estimate the effect of within-country migration on antenatal care continuity. Migrant women attended 1.8 fewer visits on average (95% CI 1.4–2.2) and were 14 percentage points less likely to complete four or more visits. The gap persists after adjustment for age, parity, education, household consumption and distance to the nearest facility, and is concentrated among households whose primary earner works in the informal sector.

We link the survey rounds to facility registers for 31 health centres and find that fewer than one in five transfers arrived with a usable antenatal record. Where a card was presented, the visit gap narrowed by roughly half, suggesting that record portability, rather than distance or cost alone, is the binding constraint.

Methods

The panel was drawn from a stratified sample of 96 enumeration areas, with oversampling of peri-urban wards where in-migration is highest. Attrition between waves was 11%; inverse-probability weights are applied throughout. Facility register linkage was carried out under a data-sharing agreement with the regional medical offices, and the analysis code is available in the replication package.

Policy implications

A portable antenatal record — digital or paper — is a low-cost intervention relative to facility construction, and the evidence here suggests it would recover a meaningful share of the lost visits. Three implementation options, with indicative costs, are set out in the accompanying brief prepared for the Ministry of Health.
